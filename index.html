AI is not just about writing code. It is about recognising patterns, asking questions, testing ideas and thinking about the people affected by technology.

Seeing AI, Seeing Ourselves is an outreach workshop series that helps school pupils explore artificial intelligence through accessible activities and discussion. Workshop 1, How Computers See, introduces how image classifiers learn from labelled examples, make predictions and sometimes get things wrong.

For teachers

Suggested classroom sequence

  1. PredictWhat do you think the AI will say?
  2. TrainWhich examples have we shown it?
  3. TestDid it get this new picture right?
  4. InvestigateDid it learn the object, or a misleading clue?
  5. ImproveWhat different training examples would help?
Under the bonnet

How the demo works

This is a simplified educational classifier, not a production computer-vision system. Each prepared picture is shrunk to 16 × 16 coloured pixels. For a new picture, the classifier finds the five most similar training pictures and lets their labels vote for cup or bottle.

The five votes show how the decision was made. They are not a percentage of how sure the model is.

Predictions change when the training examples change. The demo is there to start a discussion, not to show how accurate or fair real-world AI systems are.

Get started

Run it in the classroom

  1. Open the demo in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, or download it and double-click the file.
  2. Choose Dataset A or Dataset B, then click Train the model.
  3. Select a new test picture, predict what the model will say, then click Run the model.
  4. Use Make a challenge to change the angle, background, size or lighting, and Test log to compare results.

Works without the internet

The demo is a single file. Once downloaded, it runs with no internet connection, account, webcam, pupil photographs or uploads, so it suits school networks.

Responsible use

Privacy and safe use

No personal data

The demo uses prepared illustrations of objects. No pupil photos, names, webcam streams or personal information are needed. Activities involving pupils, consent or feedback should follow the school’s and university’s safeguarding, data-protection and research-ethics arrangements.

An educational demonstration

This is not a validated AI product. The exercise is not evidence of the fairness or accuracy of real-world image-recognition systems.
